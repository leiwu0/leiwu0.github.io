LLM pretraining: modeling, prediction, and control

Research overview · Talk slides

My goal is to make LLM pretraining more predictable and efficient. Inspired by aerodynamics, I combine controlled experiments, empirical modeling, and mathematical analysis to identify key macroscopic variables and formulate effective equations that describe training dynamics in these variables.

Current questions

Predicting training across scales

How can small-scale experiments predict a larger training run? This involves fitting models of loss dynamics, testing how their predictions transfer across scales, and identifying when the models need to be refined. Functional scaling laws provide one framework for connecting training schedules to loss trajectories.

Identifying the variables that govern training

Which macroscopic variables capture training progress and its response to optimization choices? Our work on effective learning rate (ELR) collapse shows that LLM loss dynamics are governed primarily by the learning rate divided by parameter norm, rather than the nominal learning rate (arXiv 2026).

Improving training methods

How do optimizer and schedule choices change training efficiency under a fixed compute budget? We study how optimizer design shapes training dynamics, including the roles of momentum (arXiv 2026) and blockwise sharpness (ICML 2025).

A concrete framework: functional scaling laws

We introduced functional scaling laws (FSL): explicit functionals mapping learning-rate and batch-size schedules to loss dynamics. Derived in power-law kernel regression, the framework separates signal learning from noise forgetting (NeurIPS 2025). Schematically,

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{E}_t &= S(t) + \int_0^t K(t-\tau)\,n(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\\[0.35em] S(t) &\sim t^{-\alpha}, \qquad K(t)\sim t^{-\nu}. \end{aligned} \]

Here \(t_k=\sum_{j=1}^k\eta_j\) is the intrinsic clock set by the learning-rate schedule. The noise intensity \(n(t)\), measured per unit intrinsic time, is modulated by learning rate and batch size. The macroscopic responses \(S\) and \(K\) capture signal learning and noise forgetting, with exponents \(\alpha\) and \(\nu\), respectively.

The framework characterizes optimal batch-size schedules (ICLR 2026) and learning-rate schedules (COLT 2026).

Ways to contribute

Students can approach these questions through several complementary kinds of work:

  • Training experiments. Design controlled comparisons, analyze training trajectories, and test predictions across scales.

  • Modeling and analysis. Identify macroscopic variables, fit effective models, and study their mechanisms and limits.

  • Optimizer development. Implement and evaluate optimization methods and training schedules, connecting their behavior to training efficiency.
