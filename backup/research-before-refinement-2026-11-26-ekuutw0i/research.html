Research

I work toward a predictive science of deep learning: explaining learning mechanisms, predicting training dynamics, and guiding algorithm design. My research moves between mathematical analysis of tractable models and large-scale experiments, with each informing and challenging the other.

LLM pretraining: modeling, prediction, and design

We seek a macroscopic description of large-scale pretraining through effective equations that capture the mechanisms governing loss dynamics. This approach helps explain training behavior and improve training efficiency.

1. Functional scaling laws (FSL). We introduced FSL: effective equations describing learning dynamics through the competition between signal learning and noise forgetting (NeurIPS 2025). We use this framework to study universality across optimizers and guide hyperparameter scaling:

  • Universality of FSL: SignSGD (NeurIPS 2026)

  • Hyperparameter scaling:

    • Momentum (arXiv 2026)

    • Batch-size schedules (ICLR 2026)

    • Learning-rate schedules (COLT 2026).

For more on FSL, see my talk slides.

2. ELR collapse. We identify the effective learning rate — the learning rate divided by parameter norm — as a more fundamental variable than the nominal learning rate for describing LLM loss dynamics (arXiv 2026).

3. Optimizer design. We develop optimizers for efficient LLM pretraining (ICML 2025, ICML 2026).

SGD dynamics: stability, sharpness, and generalization

Why does SGD often find minima that generalize well, even when the model is overparameterized? We introduced dynamical stability as a mechanism for SGD's implicit regularization. Our work links stability constraints and SGD's noise geometry to minima selection, sharpness, and generalization.

  • Dynamical stability and minima selection (NeurIPS 2018, NeurIPS 2022).

  • Stability and generalization (ICML 2023).

  • Noise geometry of SGD (NeurIPS 2022, TMLR 2026).

For an overview of this line of work, see my talk slides.

Function space theory of neural networks: approximation and estimation

We develop high-dimensional function-space theories of neural networks to characterize the functions they can efficiently approximate and learn. Our work establishes direct and inverse approximation theorems and quantifies approximation rates and statistical estimation errors.

  • Barron spaces: approximation and embedding theorems (Constructive Approximation 2021, JML 2023 ).

  • Approximation–estimation duality (AoS 2025).

  • Approximation separations between neural networks and linear methods (JMLR 2022).

  • Inductive biases and sample efficiency of deep CNNs (NeurIPS 2023).

For more on approximation–estimation duality, see my talk slides.
