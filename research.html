Research: Toward a predictive science of deep learning

My goal is to uncover the principles governing deep learning and use them to explain mechanisms, make quantitative predictions, and inform algorithm design.

Research perspective: scales and effective theories

My approach draws on three complementary perspectives:

  • Physics. Finding the right level of description: deciding which details matter at a given scale and building toy models to isolate the underlying mechanisms.

  • Mathematics. Establishing rigorous results under explicit conditions and clarifying the scope of a theory.

  • Engineering. Stress-testing theory in real-world systems and refining it through discrepancies between predictions and observations.

Inspired by physics, I view scale as an organizing principle for deep learning. I do not expect a single theory to capture learning across all scales; different scales require different effective theories.

This perspective suggests two complementary routes toward understanding deep learning:

  • Bottom-up. Starting from simple models and microscopic mechanisms and working toward more complex learning systems.

  • Top-down. Starting from macroscopic laws and using them to guide the study of underlying mechanisms and intermediate-scale dynamics.

Much of my earlier work followed the bottom-up route, but connecting simplified models to real-world training often proved difficult. Different assumptions and regimes can lead to very different conclusions, without revealing which mechanisms matter in practice. I am now exploring the top-down route: using macroscopic regularities to guide the construction and analysis of microscopic models, so that the two approaches can inform one another.

Functional scaling laws: toward an effective theory of LLM pretraining

We introduced functional scaling laws (FSL): explicit functionals mapping learning-rate and batch-size schedules to loss trajectories. Schematically,

\[ \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_t}_{\text{loss dynamics}} \approx \mathcal{F}_t\!\left[ \underbrace{\eta(\cdot)}_{\text{learning-rate schedule}},\, \underbrace{B(\cdot)}_{\text{batch-size schedule}} \right]. \]

Derived in power-law kernel regression, FSL reveals how signal learning and noise forgetting jointly shape learning dynamics. We use these mechanisms to predict and steer LLM pretraining.

  • Functional scaling laws. We developed the FSL framework and used it to characterize optimal learning-rate and batch-size schedules: NeurIPS 2025, ICLR 2026, COLT 2026.

  • ELR collapse. We systematically show that the loss dynamics of LLM pretraining are governed primarily by the effective learning rate (ELR), the ratio of learning rate to parameter norm, rather than the nominal learning rate: arXiv 2026.

  • Optimizer mechanisms and design. We study how optimizer design shapes training dynamics and efficiency, with recent work on momentum arXiv 2026 and blockwise sharpness ICML 2025.

For an overview, see my talk slides.

Broader research context

Large language model (LLM) pretraining optimizes a next-token prediction objective at an enormous scale:

\[ \min_{\theta} \mathcal{R}(\theta),\qquad \mathcal{R}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{D}} \left[-\sum_{k=2}^{L}\log P_{\theta}(x_k\mid x_1,\dots,x_{k-1})\right]. \]

Here, \(P_\theta\) is a Transformer language model, and \(\mathcal{D}\) is an internet-scale text corpus. Despite the complexity of this process, pretraining exhibits simple macroscopic regularities, such as scaling laws. I study these regularities at three connected levels:

  • Macroscopic laws. Identify the variables that capture training progress and the quantitative laws relating them across scales.

  • Microscopic mechanisms. Understand how these laws emerge from high-dimensional learning dynamics.

  • Training principles. Use these insights to guide optimizer and architecture design, as well as hyperparameter selection, as training scales up.

Dynamical stability of SGD

We pioneered the dynamical-stability perspective on SGD's implicit regularization to understand why SGD favors some minima over others. Our work connects the dynamical stability of minima to their sharpness and generalization properties.

  • Minima selection through dynamical stability. NeurIPS 2018.

  • SGD noise alignment and flat-minimum selection. NeurIPS 2022.

  • Stability-induced implicit regularization and generalization. ICML 2023.

  • The noise geometry of SGD. TMLR 2026.

For an overview, see my talk slides.

Barron spaces and neural-network function spaces

We introduced and named Barron spaces for two-layer neural networks, establishing approximation and generalization results within this function-space framework. Our broader work studies how neural networks efficiently approximate and learn high-dimensional functions, and how their capabilities differ from kernel and random-feature methods.

  • Barron spaces and flow-induced function spaces: direct and inverse approximation theorems. Constructive Approximation 2021.

  • Two-layer neural networks versus linear methods. JMLR 2022.

  • Inductive biases in deep convolutional networks. NeurIPS 2023.

  • A duality framework linking approximation and estimation. AoS 2025.
